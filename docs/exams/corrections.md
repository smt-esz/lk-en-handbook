# How to read my corrections

Every mark on your paper means something specific. These are the official symbols used in the final examination, so you may as well learn them now rather than in Year 12.

!!! tip "At a glance"

    |   |   |
    |---|---|
    | **Format** | Symbols in the **margin** say what kind of problem. Marks **in the text** say where and how bad |
    | **Timing** | class exams come back within three weeks. Final examination scripts you see at the official viewing session, not before |
    | **What counts** | ── straight line = language error · ∼∼ wavy line = weak expression · ✓ on a line = repeated error |
    | **Watch out** | The **wavy line** is the one that decides your band. Content problems are only ever marked in the margin |

## Marks inside the text

English has its own conventions on top of the general ones. Three marks, and the difference between them matters.

| Mark | Meaning | What to do about it |
|---|---|---|
| ── straight line | A **language error**: grammar, lexis, spelling, punctuation | Something is wrong. Work out what, look it up if needed |
| ∼∼ wavy line | **Weak expression**. Not wrong, but imprecise, unidiomatic, flat or repetitive | This is where range lives. Find a better word or structure |
| ✓ tick on a line | A **repeated error** or one that follows from an earlier mistake | Counted once, but it tells you this is your personal pattern |

The wavy line is the one to take seriously. It is not "almost correct", it is the mark that separates a mid-band text from a top-band one, because **range beats accuracy** in the marking scheme. If your paper comes back with few straight lines and many wavy ones, your problem is not carelessness, it is vocabulary and sentence variety.

The tick is a gift. Collect them across a term and you have a personal error list worth more than any general grammar book.

## Symbols in the margin: language and form

The letters are abbreviations of the official German terms. You will see them on every marked paper, so the German is worth knowing even though you never write it yourself.

| Symbol | Stands for | Meaning | What it usually means for you |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Ausdruck | Expression | The words are wrong for the job, not ungrammatical |
| **Gr** | Grammatik | Grammar | Tense, agreement, articles, word forms |
| **S** | Satzbau | Sentence structure | Word order, clause linking, run-on sentences |
| **R** | Rechtschreibung | Spelling | Look it up and add it to your error log |
| **Z** | Zeichensetzung | Punctuation | Commas before relative clauses are the usual suspect |
| **ul** | unleserlich | Illegible | Not marked at all. Unreadable is unmarked |

## Symbols in the margin: content

The general symbol is **I** for *Inhalt*, content. Where it helps, it is made more precise:

| Symbol | Stands for | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Th** | Thema verfehlt | Task or topic not addressed |
| **Bg** | Begründung | Reason missing or wrong |
| **Bl** | Beleg | Evidence from the material missing |
| **Bp** | Beispiel | Example missing or unsuitable |
| **Df** | Definition | Definition wrong |
| **Fs** | Fachsprache | Breach of subject terminology |
| **Lg** | Logik | Logic |
| **W** | Wiederholung | Unjustified repetition, long-windedness |
| **Zs** | Zusammenhang | Break in the line of thought |
| **f** | falsch | Wrong |
| **ug** | ungenau | Imprecise |
| **uv** | unvollständig | Incomplete |

**Bl** and **Bp** are the two that cost most marks in analyses. Both mean the same underlying thing: you asserted something without anchoring it in the text.

## Colours

In the final examination, three different people may write on your paper.

| Colour | Who | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Red | First marker, your own teacher | Right margin |
| Green | Second marker, a teacher from another school | Left margin |
| Brown | Third marker, if the first two disagree | Left margin, sets the final symbol |

You will not see points or assessment units written on a final examination script. Markers are not allowed to enter them on the paper.

## Working with a corrected exam

1. **Sort before you despair.** Count straight lines, wavy lines and ticks separately. They point to three different problems with three different fixes.
2. **Take the ticks first.** Repeated errors are the cheapest points on the board.
3. **Rewrite one paragraph, not the whole text.** Pick the paragraph with the most wavy lines and rewrite it with better range. That is the exercise that actually moves a band.
4. **Keep an error log.** One page per term, three columns: what I wrote, what is correct, why.
5. **Bring it to me.** You can hand in a rewritten passage for oral feedback at any time and it is not graded.
