# What actually gets asked

Seventeen past papers, from 2007 to 2023, all Advanced Course, all first sitting. The paper changes topic every year and its shape barely changes at all. This page is what that shape looks like, so that nothing on the day is a surprise.

!!! tip "At a glance"

    |   |   |
    |---|---|
    | **Format** | Part A1 two set operators, Part A2 choose one of three, Part B mediation with no choice |
    | **Timing** | 285 minutes for everything, choice and reading included |
    | **What counts** | A1 and A2 are marked together as the writing task. Sub-task weightings are printed on the paper |
    | **Watch out** | The three A2 options are **not** equally quick. Read all three before choosing |

## Part A1: working with the text

Always two instructions, and in seventeen years they never once asked for only one. The first is comprehension, the second is analysis.

| Typical first operator | Typical second operator |
|---|---|
| <ul><li>Outline …</li><li>Sum up …</li><li>Point out …</li><li>Explain how …</li><li>Describe …</li></ul> | <ul><li>Analyse how … is conveyed</li><li>Analyse and compare how both texts …</li><li>Examine the way the author …</li><li>Analyse the presentation of …</li></ul> |

Two things follow from this. The first instruction wants **the text's content in your words**, not your reading of it. The second wants **how**, which means [statement, evidence, effect](../analysis/non-fictional-texts.md) every time. Answering the second operator in the mode of the first is the single most common way to lose content marks here.

Occasionally A1 gives you **two texts** to compare. The comparison is then part of the operator, not an optional extra.

## Part A2: choose one of three

This is where the paper offers you a decision, and it is worth understanding what you are choosing between. Across the years the three options fall into recognizable types.

| Type | How often | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Comment or discussion on a statement** | Every year | A quotation, a proverb or a claim, and an instruction to comment, discuss or assess |
| **Visual as a starting point** | Roughly two years in three | A cartoon, photograph or advertisement. Analyse briefly, then comment on its message |
| **Text type in a situation** | Every year | You are given a role, an addressee and a purpose, and asked to write a speech, article, blog entry, letter or email |

### The situations that keep coming back

Drawn from the papers themselves, so these are the roles you should have practised:

- Write a **speech** for a public speaking course, a youth conference or a school event
- Write an **article** for a project website, a magazine or a school paper
- Write a **blog entry** for a named blog, responding to a statement from the text
- Write a **letter to the editor** of a newspaper, defending or attacking a position
- Write an **email** to a project group, recommending one of two cartoons for a campaign
- Write a **text for the school website** advertising a campaign

Notice what dominates: **speech, article, blog entry**. If you have practised those three properly, you have covered most of what the third option has asked for over a decade.

### How to choose in the exam

You have about 20 minutes of reading and choice time. Spend three of them here.

1. **Read all three.** The shortest-looking one is usually the comment, and a comment with nothing to say about it is not quick.
2. **Ask which one you have material for.** Background knowledge from the topic fields shows immediately in the comment options.
3. **Ask which text type you have actually written before.** The situation tasks are marked on text type conventions as well as on content. An unfamiliar format costs marks you cannot recover with good ideas.
4. **Check what the visual option really wants.** "Based on a short analysis of the cartoon, comment on its message" is two tasks in one. Budget for both.
5. **Decide and commit.** Switching after fifteen minutes is how people run out of time.

## Part B: mediation

No choice, one task, and the shape is consistent: a German source text and a **situation** that tells you who you are writing for and why.

Recurring set-ups from the papers:

- You are taking part in an **international youth conference** and share an example from your own country on the conference website
- You write an **article for a project website** using information from the source
- You write for an English-speaking audience who need a German idea explained

That means two things are always being tested beyond your English. **Selection**: the task decides what is relevant, not the text. **Cultural explanation**: a German institution dropped in without a gloss loses content marks.

## The instructions on the front page

Printed on every paper, in German, and worth knowing before the day:

- **You may do the parts in any order.** A1, A2 and B, in whatever sequence suits you
- **Never more than four consecutive words** unchanged from the source. Quotations must be marked as such
- **Label the parts clearly** and keep them separate: A1, A2 and B
- Permitted aids: **mono- and bilingual dictionaries** plus a German spelling dictionary

## A caution about old papers

The papers up to 2023 print an assessment grid in **assessment units**, split into content richness, linguistic correctness and expression. **That grid no longer applies.** From 2025 the Advanced Course in modern foreign languages is marked in grade points, language 60 per cent and content 40 per cent, along the criteria described under [Exams and grading](exams-grading.md).

Use the old papers for what they are excellent for: **the tasks**. Ignore their marking grids.

## How to use a past paper

- [ ] Read the whole paper first, including all three A2 options
- [ ] Time yourself from the first glance, not from when you start writing
- [ ] Write A1 and A2 in one sitting. Splitting them hides your real time problem
- [ ] Do at least one full paper, all 285 minutes, before the mock exam
- [ ] Write at least one mediation from a German newspaper text that nobody has pre-selected for you
- [ ] After each attempt, mark only your own language: count the wavy lines you would have earned
