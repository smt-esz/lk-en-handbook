# Exams & grading

How written work is graded in this course. The system is the one the final examination uses, so everything here still applies in year 12. German terms are kept where they are the official ones.

!!! tip "At a glance"

    |   |   |
    |---|---|
    | **Format** | grade points from 0 to 15, never assessment units. Language and content marked separately |
    | **Timing** | class exams up to 180 minutes. Once per subject the full examination time of 285 minutes. Returned within three weeks |
    | **What counts** | **Language 60 %, content 40 %.** Language in three equal categories: lexis, grammar, text organization |
    | **Watch out** | **Range beats accuracy.** Never copy more than four consecutive words. "Ungenügend" in either half caps that part at three points |

## How many exams?

| | First term | Second term |
|---|---|---|
| Year 11 | 1–2 class exams + other assessment | 1–2 class exams + other assessment |
| Year 12 | 1–2 class exams + other assessment | optional 1 class exam + other assessment |

The exact number for each term, and the weighting of class exams against other assessment, is announced at the start of the school year. Both are decided by the English department.

**Other assessment** can include:

- vocabulary tests
- text production
- oral contribution in class
- presentations, debates
- podcasts or vlogs
- reading journals
- long-term assignments

## The exam format

Class exams in a foreign language may run up to **180 minutes**. Once per subject a class exam may take the full examination time of **285 minutes**, and we will use that in 12/I so that you have written the real thing before the real thing.

Class exams mirror the examination tasks: a text-based writing task (analysis plus composition) and, regularly, a mediation. There are no listening comprehension tasks in the Advanced Course. See [What actually gets asked](task-formats.md) for the shape of the paper.

## How your grade is built

Every written task is marked in **grade points from 0 to 15**, not in assessment units. Language and content are marked separately and then combined.

```
Language  ×  0.6
Content   ×  0.4
──────────────────
=  grade points for the task
```

Two consequences worth sitting with:

- **Language is the bigger half.** A brilliant argument in narrow, error-prone English will not reach the top band. Revising your sentences is not cosmetic work, it is the majority of the marks.
- **Content is marked per sub-task, language for the whole task at once.** A weak paragraph 3 drags down the content marks for paragraph 3. Sloppy English anywhere drags down the language marks everywhere.

## Language: the three categories

They carry **equal weight**. A strong one does not compensate a weak one.

| Category | What is judged |
|---|---|
| **Lexis** | *Range*: precise and varied word choice, idiomatic expressions. *Accuracy*: correctness, comprehensibility |
| **Grammar** | *Range*: varied sentence structure, structures used for a purpose. *Accuracy*: correctness, comprehensibility |
| **Text organization** | *Structure*: [cohesion](../language/paragraph-structure.md), so connectives, referring back, repeating words and structures, paragraphing. *Appropriateness*: text type conventions, [register](../language/register-and-tone.md) fitting situation and addressee, independent phrasing |

### Range beats accuracy

The single most misunderstood rule in the course. From the official marking guidance:

> Range is the decisive factor when judging grammar and lexis, which means insufficient range cannot be compensated by a high degree of accuracy.

In plain terms: nothing but short, safe, correct sentences with common vocabulary puts a ceiling on your mark. Take controlled risks. A subordinate clause you get slightly wrong is worth more than a main clause you avoided.

### Spelling is not its own category

Orthography is marked inside lexis and grammar, under accuracy. It matters, but it is not a separate column, and perfect spelling does not rescue a text with no range.

## Content: what is judged

| Task | Categories |
|---|---|
| **Summary / first sub-task** | Task fulfilment according to the operator; focus on relevant aspects; level of abstraction where required; structure |
| **Analysis** | Task fulfilment; interpretation of the text taking its stylistic devices into account; reference to the text with apt, differentiated and functional evidence and quotations; structure |
| **Comment / personal response** | Task fulfilment; argumentation developing a position of your own; reference to material and topic with fitting examples and evidence; structure |
| **Creative writing** | Task fulfilment; convincing ideas realized in the text type, with a clear sense of situation and addressee; reference to material and topic; structure |
| **Mediation** | Task fulfilment according to the operator; focus on relevant aspects, abstraction and cultural explanation where required; situation and addressee realized in the text type; structure |

"Structure" appears in every row. Clear, non-redundant, coherent organization earns marks in its own right on every task you will write here.

## The four-word rule

!!! warning "Never copy more than four consecutive words"

    In text production you may not take **more than four words in a row** unchanged from the source. Quotations used deliberately and the topic vocabulary contained in the text are exempt.

The most common avoidable mistake in summaries and analyses. If you are keeping five or six words of the original because "it says it best", that is exactly the moment to paraphrase.

## The three-point cap

A performance rated "unsatisfactory" in **either** language **or** content limits that part of the exam to three points in simple weighting, however good the other half is. You cannot pass a task on content alone, and you cannot pass it on English alone.

## Answers that miss the task

Parts of an answer with no linguistic or thematic connection to the task set are not marked at all. They are flagged and treated as unwritten. Writing more is not a strategy.

## Before you hand in any written task

- [ ] Have I answered the operator, or something adjacent to it?
- [ ] Does every claim have evidence from the text?
- [ ] Have I checked for runs of more than four words taken from the source?
- [ ] Have I varied my connectives, or does every paragraph start the same way?
- [ ] Have I taken any risks with structure and vocabulary, or played it safe throughout?
- [ ] One pass for tense, one for agreement, one for spelling
- [ ] Is the structure visible: paragraphs, [topic sentences](../language/paragraph-structure.md), a real conclusion?

## Getting feedback

You can hand in text productions for oral feedback at any time. This is practice, it is not graded, and it is the cheapest way to move a category up a band.
