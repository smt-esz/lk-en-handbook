# Exam day

The practical rules for a written examination day. None of this is about English, all of it can cost you the exam. Read it once in Year 11 and again the week before.

!!! tip "At a glance"

    |   |   |
    |---|---|
    | **Format** | Fair copy on white sheets, one side only. Draft on grey sheets, both sides. A cover sheet records the count |
    | **Timing** | Working time starts when the tasks are handed out and includes reading and choosing |
    | **What counts** | Only the **fair copy** is marked. School code top left, candidate number top right, on every sheet |
    | **Watch out** | **No sheet may carry your name.** Blue or black only, no correction pens. Checking your aids is your responsibility |

## What you take in

| Take in | Leave outside |
|---|---|
| Pens, blue or black | Bags and coats |
| Permitted aids (dictionaries) | Any communication device, phone switched off on the teacher's desk |
| Pencil for drawings | Your own paper of any kind |
| Drinks, sandwiches, fruit | Correction pens or tape |
| Tissues in a **closed, unopened** pack | Notes of any kind |

The seating plan is drawn by lot, is binding, and goes into the minutes.

## Paper and identification

You are given a folder with white sheets for the **fair copy** and grey sheets for the **draft**, plus a cover sheet.

- Every sheet you use: **school code** top left, **candidate number** top right
- **No sheet may contain your name.** The second marker must not know whose paper it is
- Number every sheet, fair copy and draft alike
- Write the total number of sheets on the cover sheet at the end
- **Fair copy: one side only. Draft: both sides**
- Hand in everything, including unused and unnumbered sheets and the task papers

## Writing conventions

- Blue or black only
- Drawings in pencil
- Correction pens are not allowed. Cross out cleanly with a ruler
- Only the **fair copy** is marked. If in an emergency a passage of your draft should count, write on it that it is to be marked as fair copy, and add a pointer to it at the right place in the fair copy

## Time

Your working time begins when the tasks are handed out. **Reading time and choosing between tasks are part of it**, not extra. For English that means 285 minutes covers everything from first glance to handing in.

Handing in on time is on you. There is no grace period.

## During the exam

- Leaving the room is signalled by raising your hand, is noted in the minutes, and happens one candidate at a time
- Keep it brief
- No contact with anyone else inside or outside the room
- You may not leave the building during exam time
- Before the exam you are asked whether you are fit to sit it. If you are not, you need a medical certificate. If you fall ill during the exam, tell the invigilator immediately

## Aids are your responsibility

The rules for dictionaries and other aids are yours to comply with. **Not complying can be treated as an attempted deception**, whatever the intention. Check your electronic dictionary the evening before: memory cleared or locked, no internet capability.

## A note on presentation

In most subjects, heavy and repeated breaches of language correctness or of formal presentation can cost one point in simple weighting. In English this is already built into the language marking, so it is not deducted twice. It is not a licence to be sloppy, it is just where the rule lives.

## The night before

- [ ] Electronic dictionary: memory cleared or locked, no internet capability
- [ ] Two or three working pens, blue or black
- [ ] Pencil and a ruler
- [ ] No correction pen anywhere in the bag
- [ ] Tissues in a sealed pack
- [ ] Something to drink and something to eat
- [ ] Alarm set with a margin. Arriving flustered costs you the first twenty minutes

## The first two minutes in the room

- [ ] Phone off and on the teacher's desk before you sit down
- [ ] School code and candidate number written on the cover sheet
- [ ] Fair-copy and draft sheets separated, white and grey
- [ ] Read the whole paper once before writing anything
- [ ] Note the percentage weightings of the sub-tasks

## Before you hand in

- [ ] Every sheet carries the school code and your candidate number
- [ ] No sheet carries your name
- [ ] All sheets numbered, fair copy and draft alike
- [ ] Sheet count entered on the cover sheet
- [ ] Anything from the draft that should count is marked, with a pointer in the fair copy
- [ ] Everything goes back in, including blank sheets and the task papers

## Results

Results of the written exams are announced individually by the chair of the examination board and the sixth-form coordinator. If a paper is marked with 0 points, you are told at that moment that an additional oral exam is necessary.
