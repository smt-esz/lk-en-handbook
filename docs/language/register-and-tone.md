# Register and tone

Register and tone are graded explicitly under text organization ("Appropriateness": register fitting situation and addressee), and naming a tone without evidence is treated the same as spotting a [stylistic device](stylistic-devices.md) without explaining it.

## Register: how formal is the language?

Register is the level of formality, not the writer's mood. Three bands cover almost everything you will read or write.

| Feature | Formal | Neutral | Informal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contractions | avoided (*do not*, *cannot*) | occasional | frequent (*don't*, *can't*) |
| Vocabulary | Latinate (*obtain*, *request*, *commence*) | everyday (*get*, *ask*, *start*) | colloquial (*grab*, *kick off*) |
| Sentence length | longer, more subordination | mixed | short, simple |
| Address | third person, no direct *you* unless the addressee is defined | some direct address | direct, personal (*you*, *we*) |
| Passive voice | more common | occasional | rare |
| Modal hedging | *one might argue*, *it could be suggested* | *I think*, *it seems* | *I reckon*, *kind of* |
| Punctuation | minimal exclamation marks | standard | exclamation marks, dashes, ellipses for effect |

**Which register for which task:** analysis and non-fiction essays are formal to neutral, third person, simple present. A comment or discussion is neutral to formal, first person allowed. A speech is neutral to formal but direct, addressing the audience as *you*/*we*. An email to a friend inside a mediation or creative task drops into informal. Mediation itself has no fixed register, the addressee and text type set it, see [How to write a mediation](../writing/mediation.md).

## Tone: what is the writer's attitude?

Tone is not how formal a text is, it is the writer's **attitude** to the subject or the reader: serious, ironic, urgent, nostalgic, sympathetic, critical, playful, indignant. A text can shift tone within a single paragraph, name the shift rather than forcing one label onto the whole piece.

**How to find it:**

- **Word choice.** Positively or negatively loaded words reveal attitude before anything else is said. *Regime* versus *government*, *slashed* versus *reduced*.
- **Stylistic devices.** Irony, hyperbole, understatement and rhetorical questions all carry tone, see [Stylistic devices](stylistic-devices.md).
- **Sentence structure.** Short, clipped sentences suggest urgency or tension. Long, flowing sentences suggest reflection or nostalgia.
- **Punctuation.** Exclamation marks signal passion or alarm, ellipses suggest hesitation or trailing off, rhetorical questions invite the reader in or challenge them.

**A word bank for naming tone:**

| Category | Words |
|---|---|
| Critical | critical, scathing, indignant, mocking, ironic, sarcastic |
| Reflective | nostalgic, wistful, contemplative, resigned, melancholic |
| Positive | hopeful, celebratory, enthusiastic, reassuring, admiring |
| Neutral / informative | matter-of-fact, objective, detached, factual |
| Urgent / persuasive | urgent, passionate, earnest, alarmed, insistent |

## Common mistakes

- Naming a tone with no evidence. Tone needs the same **statement → evidence (quote + line) → effect** as any other claim.
- Using "formal" or "informal" to describe tone. Those words describe register, not attitude.
- Picking one tone word for a whole text that actually shifts, for example from nostalgic to urgent in the final paragraph.
