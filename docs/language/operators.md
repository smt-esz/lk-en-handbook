# Operators: what each task word means

The operator tells you exactly what kind of answer is expected. Doing the wrong operation is one of the most expensive mistakes in a class exam: an *analysis* that only *describes* loses points even if everything in it is correct.

Operators come in three levels (Anforderungsbereiche): **AFB I** reproduction, **AFB II** analysis and reorganization, **AFB III** evaluation and production.

!!! tip "At a glance"

    |   |   |
    |---|---|
    | **Purpose** | Work out what operation a task word actually demands before you start writing |
    | **Structure** | Grouped by AFB I, II and III, the three levels of demand |
    | **Register & voice** | Not set by the operator. The text type decides that |
    | **Length** | AFB I answers are short, AFB III answers carry the most weight |
    | **Watch out** | Doing the **wrong operation** is the most expensive mistake in a class exam. An analysis that only describes loses marks even when everything in it is true |

## AFB I: Understanding and reproducing

| Operator | What you have to do |
|---|---|
| describe | Say in detail what something is like, without interpreting it |
| outline | Give the main features or the structure, no details |
| point out | Identify and briefly explain selected aspects |
| present | (Re)structure information and lay it out clearly |
| state | Say something clearly and briefly, no discussion |
| summarize / sum up | Give a concise account of the main points in your own words |

## AFB II: Analysing and restructuring

| Operator | What you have to do |
|---|---|
| analyse | Break something down into its parts and examine how they work and relate; always with evidence and effect |
| characterize | Describe and examine a person's traits, behaviour and development |
| compare | Point out similarities **and** differences, ordered by aspects |
| contrast | Focus on the differences between two things |
| examine | Look closely at one aspect and investigate it in detail |
| explain | Make something clear by giving reasons or background |
| illustrate | Make something clear by using examples |
| relate | Show connections between two things or texts |

## AFB III: Evaluating and creating

| Operator | What you have to do |
|---|---|
| assess | Consider arguments for and against and reach a balanced judgement |
| comment (on) | Give your own opinion on a defined issue, supported by arguments |
| discuss | Look at both sides of a question, weigh them, reach a verdict |
| evaluate | Form an opinion after carefully weighing advantages and disadvantages |
| interpret | Explain the deeper meaning of a text or its elements in a wider context |
| justify | Give solid reasons for a position or decision |
| write (a letter, speech, article …) | Produce the required text type, observing its conventions |

!!! note "Read the whole task, not just the operator"

    "Analyse how the author creates suspense" limits the analysis to suspense. The operator sets the operation; the rest of the task sets the focus. Answer exactly that, nothing else.

## Operators that get confused

These pairs look similar but ask for a different operation. Mixing them up is the exact mistake this page exists to prevent.

- **describe vs. analyse:** *describe* stays with what is there, *analyse* asks why it works and with what effect
- **discuss vs. comment:** *discuss* weighs both sides before reaching a verdict, *comment* argues your own position from the start
- **characterize vs. describe:** *characterize* is for people, their traits and development; *describe* covers anything, including settings and objects
- **examine vs. analyse:** *examine* stays with one aspect and goes deep, *analyse* breaks the whole thing into its parts
- **interpret vs. analyse:** *interpret* reaches for the deeper meaning in a wider context, *analyse* stays with evidence and effect in the text itself
