# False friends and German habits

Two categories of mistake that cost more marks than they should, because they are invisible to the person making them. A false friend feels right. A German sentence pattern feels natural. Both land under **lexis** and **grammar**, and both are exactly what the mediation task is designed to catch.

!!! tip "At a glance"

    |   |   |
    |---|---|
    | **Purpose** | Catch the errors that feel correct to a German speaker |
    | **Structure** | False friends first, then sentence patterns, then the small words |
    | **Register & voice** | Everything here applies to every text type you write |
    | **Length** | A lookup page. Take three entries at a time |
    | **Watch out** | These errors do not feel wrong while you write. Only a checking pass finds them |

## Why this page exists

The marking scheme judges lexis and grammar on **range** and **accuracy** separately, and it judges mediation additionally on whether your English reads as English. German sentence patterns showing through are named explicitly as something to avoid. A text that is grammatically defensible but audibly German loses marks in two categories at once.

## German sentence patterns that show through

These are not vocabulary errors. Every word is English and the sentence still sounds German.

| Pattern | Sounds German | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| Verb second after an adverbial | Yesterday went I to the library. | Yesterday **I went** to the library. |
| Verb at the end of a subordinate clause | … because he the answer knew. | … because he **knew** the answer. |
| *Since* for a period | I live here since three years. | I **have lived** here **for** three years. |
| *Until* for a deadline | I finish it until Friday. | I will finish it **by** Friday. |
| *Make* for *do* | I have to make my homework. | I have to **do** my homework. |
| *Become* for *get* | He became a present. | He **got** a present. |
| Missing *do* in questions | Know you the answer? | **Do you know** the answer? |
| *Information* as a plural | The informations are helpful. | The **information is** helpful. |
| Article with abstract nouns | The life is difficult. | **Life** is difficult. |
| *Also* for *so* | Also, we decided to leave. | **So** we decided to leave. |
| *Actually* for *currently* | Actually he lives in Leeds. | **At present** he lives in Leeds. |
| Present for future after *if* | If it will rain, we stay in. | If it **rains**, we will stay in. |
| Comma before *that* | He said, that he was late. | He said **that** he was late. |
| *In the year 2020* | In the year 2020 the law changed. | **In 2020** the law changed. |
| Double negative from *nicht mehr* | He does not work not anymore. | He **no longer** works there. |

## Small words that give you away

| Confusion | The rule in one line |
|---|---|
| **since / for** | *Since* takes a point in time, *for* takes a stretch of time |
| **its / it's** | *It's* is only ever *it is* or *it has* |
| **who / which** | *Who* for people, *which* for things, *that* for either in defining clauses |
| **fewer / less** | *Fewer* for things you can count, *less* for things you cannot |
| **each other / one another** | Interchangeable in modern English. Do not agonize |
| **economic / economical** | *Economic* relates to the economy, *economical* means thrifty |
| **historic / historical** | *Historic* means important, *historical* means from the past |
| **effect / affect** | *Effect* is nearly always the noun, *affect* the verb |
| **lay / lie** | You *lie* down, you *lay* something down |
| **borrow / lend** | You *borrow* from, you *lend* to |
| **say / tell** | You *say* something, you *tell* someone |
| **learn / teach** | Students *learn*, teachers *teach* |

## Punctuation, briefly

German comma rules do not transfer. Three that turn up constantly:

- **No comma before *that*.** *He argued that the law was unfair.*
- **A comma before a non-defining relative clause, none before a defining one.** *My brother, who lives in Kiel, is a teacher* has one brother. *My brother who lives in Kiel* has more than one.
- **Quotation marks and full stops.** In British English the full stop goes outside unless it belongs to the quotation.

## How to actually fix this

Reading a list does not change your writing. Two things do.

1. **Keep an error log.** One page per term. Three columns: what I wrote, what is correct, why. When the same entry appears twice, that is your personal pattern and it is worth ten minutes.
2. **Do a dedicated checking pass.** Not a general reread. One pass looking only for the four or five things on your list. False friends do not feel wrong while you write, which is exactly why they survive a normal reread.
