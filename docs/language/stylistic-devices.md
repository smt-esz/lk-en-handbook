# Stylistic devices

For every device you name in an analysis: quote it, give the line, explain the **effect in context**. The "typical effect" column is a starting point, not a ready-made answer.

!!! tip "At a glance"

    |   |   |
    |---|---|
    | **Purpose** | Name a device correctly and, more importantly, say what it does |
    | **Structure** | Grouped by what the device works on: imagery, sound, structure, argument |
    | **Register & voice** | Analysis is formal, neutral perspective, simple present |
    | **Length** | Two or three well-explained devices beat a list of ten |
    | **Watch out** | **Statement → evidence (quote + line) → effect.** A device spotted but not explained earns nothing |

## Imagery

| Device | Definition | Example | Typical effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simile | Explicit comparison using *like* or *as* | I wandered lonely as a cloud (Wordsworth) | Makes an idea vivid by linking it to something familiar |
| Metaphor | Implicit comparison; one thing *is* another | All the world's a stage (Shakespeare) | Compresses meaning; shapes how we see the subject |
| Personification | Human qualities given to things or ideas | The wind whispered through the trees | Brings scenes to life; creates atmosphere |
| Symbol | Concrete object standing for an abstract idea | The dove stands for peace | Loads a simple image with larger meaning |
| Hyperbole | Deliberate exaggeration | I've told you a million times | Adds emphasis or humour; signals strong emotion |
| Understatement | Deliberate downplaying | "Not bad" for an outstanding result | Irony, modesty, dry humour |
| Euphemism | Mild expression for something harsh | He passed away (= died) | Softens; can also veil or manipulate |

## Sound

| Device | Definition | Example | Typical effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alliteration | Same initial consonant in neighbouring words | Safe and sound | Catchy, memorable; binds words together |
| Assonance | Repeated vowel sounds | The rain in Spain stays mainly in the plain | Musical quality; slows or smooths the line |
| Onomatopoeia | Words that imitate sounds | buzz, crash, hiss | Makes description sensory and immediate |
| Rhyme | Identical end sounds | night / light | Structure, memorability, harmony |

## Structure

| Device | Definition | Example | Typical effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anaphora | Repetition at the start of successive sentences or lines | We shall fight on the beaches, we shall fight on the landing grounds … (Churchill) | Builds rhythm and intensity; hammers a point home |
| Repetition | Repeating a word or phrase | Never, never, never give up | Emphasis; makes the key idea stick |
| Parallelism | Same grammatical structure in successive parts | Easy come, easy go | Balance and clarity; invites comparison |
| Rule of three | Three parallel elements | blood, sweat and tears | Feels complete and forceful; highly memorable |
| Enumeration | Listing of items | He packed shirts, books, hopes and doubts | Creates abundance or urgency; can build to a climax |
| Climax | Arrangement from weakest to strongest | He came, he saw, he conquered | Rising intensity toward the key point |
| Anticlimax | Sudden drop from important to trivial | He lost his empire, his army and his umbrella | Deflates; often humorous or satirical |
| Antithesis | Opposed ideas in parallel form | One small step for man, one giant leap for mankind (Armstrong) | Sharpens contrast; memorable phrasing |
| Inversion | Unusual word order | Never have I seen such courage | Emphasizes the fronted element; formal or dramatic tone |
| Chiasmus | Second phrase mirrors the first | Ask not what your country can do for you; ask what you can do for your country (Kennedy) | Elegant reversal; makes the idea feel conclusive |

## Meaning and attitude

| Device | Definition | Example | Typical effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Irony | Saying the opposite of what is meant | "Lovely weather" during a storm | Distance, humour, criticism between the lines |
| Sarcasm | Aggressive, mocking irony | "Well done" after a blunder | Ridicules a person or position |
| Satire | Exposing folly through humour and exaggeration | A cartoon showing politicians as squabbling children | Criticizes by making the target look absurd |
| Rhetorical question | Question with an implied answer | Who doesn't want to be happy? | Involves the audience; steers them to agree |
| Allusion | Brief reference to a well-known text, event or figure | A modern "David versus Goliath" story | Activates shared knowledge; adds depth quickly |
| Oxymoron | Two contradictory terms combined | deafening silence | Startles; captures a paradox in two words |
| Paradox | Statement that seems absurd but holds truth | Less is more | Provokes thought; reveals hidden truth |
| Pun | Play on words with double meaning | Time flies like an arrow; fruit flies like a banana | Humour; shows wit; grabs attention |
| Metonymy | Substituting an associated term | The White House announced … | Compact; shifts focus to the institution or aspect |
| Synecdoche | Part stands for whole (or vice versa) | All hands on deck | Vivid compression |
