# Useful phrases for writing

!!! tip "At a glance"

    |   |   |
    |---|---|
    | **Purpose** | Find the phrase for the job you need done in a sentence |
    | **Structure** | One table per function. Left column neutral, right column more formal or more careful |
    | **Register & voice** | Everything here works in essays, comments, analyses and mediations. Check before using it in an informal email |
    | **Length** | Two or three connectives per paragraph is plenty |
    | **Watch out** | Range is marked. **Vary your connectives**, and never open three paragraphs in a row the same way |

Phrases are tools, not filler. A connective that does not describe a real relationship between two ideas costs marks under text organization, see [Paragraph structure and cohesion](paragraph-structure.md).

## Opening a text

| Naming the topic | Framing the question |
|---|---|
| <ul><li>This essay discusses …</li><li>This text deals with …</li><li>The following will look at …</li><li>The subject of this comment is …</li></ul> | <ul><li>The question of whether … has been widely debated.</li><li>The issue at the heart of this text is …</li><li>Few topics divide opinion as sharply as …</li><li>Recent debate has centred on the question of …</li></ul> |

## Announcing your line of thought

| Straightforward | More formal |
|---|---|
| <ul><li>In the following, I will examine …</li><li>I will first look at …, then …</li><li>This text argues that …</li></ul> | <ul><li>This essay sets out to demonstrate that …</li><li>The following analysis proceeds in three steps.</li><li>It will be argued that …</li></ul> |

## Adding and sequencing

| Adding a point | Ordering points |
|---|---|
| <ul><li>Furthermore, …</li><li>Moreover, …</li><li>In addition, …</li><li>What is more, …</li><li>Another important point is …</li><li>Equally important is …</li><li>Apart from that, …</li></ul> | <ul><li>First … / Second … / Finally, …</li><li>To begin with, …</li><li>The next point to consider is …</li><li>More importantly, …</li><li>Most significantly of all, …</li><li>Last but by no means least, …</li></ul> |

!!! warning "Not interchangeable"

    *Furthermore* adds weight to the same line of argument. *Another point is* opens a new one. Markers notice when a connective claims a relationship that the sentences do not have.

## Contrasting and qualifying

| Full contrast | Partial contrast |
|---|---|
| <ul><li>However, …</li><li>By contrast, …</li><li>On the other hand, …</li><li>Conversely, …</li><li>Whereas … / While …</li><li>Unlike …, …</li></ul> | <ul><li>Nevertheless, …</li><li>Nonetheless, …</li><li>Despite this, …</li><li>Then again, …</li><li>That said, …</li><li>At the same time, …</li></ul> |

## Conceding a point before you answer it

| Conceding | Turning back |
|---|---|
| <ul><li>Admittedly, …</li><li>It is true that …</li><li>Granted, …</li><li>There is no denying that …</li><li>Critics rightly point out that …</li></ul> | <ul><li>… , but this overlooks …</li><li>This argument does not hold, however, because …</li><li>What this misses is …</li><li>Even so, …</li><li>This objection loses much of its force once …</li></ul> |

Concession is the cheapest way to show you have thought about the other side. Pair it every time: concede, then turn.

## Giving examples and evidence

| Introducing an example | Pointing to the text |
|---|---|
| <ul><li>For example, … / For instance, …</li><li>… , such as …</li><li>A case in point is …</li><li>To illustrate this, …</li><li>Take … as an example.</li></ul> | <ul><li>This can be seen in line 12, where …</li><li>The author makes this explicit when they write, "…" (l. 7).</li><li>… illustrates this when …</li><li>The phrase "…" (l. 23) shows that …</li><li>This is borne out by …</li></ul> |

## Cause, effect and inference

| Cause and effect | Drawing a conclusion from evidence |
|---|---|
| <ul><li>Therefore, … / Thus, …</li><li>As a result, …</li><li>Consequently, …</li><li>This leads to … / This results in …</li><li>For that reason, …</li><li>… , which is why …</li></ul> | <ul><li>This shows that …</li><li>It can be seen that …</li><li>This suggests that …</li><li>The implication is that …</li><li>From this it follows that …</li><li>This creates the impression that …</li></ul> |

!!! tip "Analysis needs the right column"

    *Therefore* links two facts. *This suggests that* links a fact to your reading of it. In an analysis you almost always want the second kind, because that is where the effect on the reader is described.

## Describing an author's moves

| Neutral reporting | Sharper reporting |
|---|---|
| <ul><li>The author states / explains / describes that …</li><li>According to the author, …</li><li>The text points out that …</li><li>… goes on to say that …</li></ul> | <ul><li>The author argues / claims / maintains that …</li><li>… emphasizes / stresses / underlines …</li><li>… criticizes / dismisses / rejects …</li><li>… concedes / admits that …</li><li>… warns against …</li><li>… appeals to the reader's sense of …</li></ul> |

Reporting verbs carry judgement. *States* is neutral, *claims* signals doubt, *concedes* signals a retreat. Choosing well is a lexis mark.

## Describing structure and technique

| Structure | Effect on the reader |
|---|---|
| <ul><li>The text falls into three parts.</li><li>The opening paragraph establishes …</li><li>The argument builds towards …</li><li>The turning point comes when …</li><li>The final paragraph returns to …</li></ul> | <ul><li>This draws the reader into …</li><li>The effect is to make the reader feel …</li><li>This lends the argument an air of …</li><li>The repetition drives home …</li><li>By addressing the reader directly, the author creates …</li></ul> |

## Expressing an opinion

| Direct | Measured |
|---|---|
| <ul><li>In my opinion, … / In my view, …</li><li>I am convinced that …</li><li>I would argue that …</li><li>To my mind, …</li><li>From my point of view, …</li></ul> | <ul><li>It seems to me that …</li><li>I would tend to agree that …</li><li>There is much to be said for …</li><li>On balance, I find … more convincing.</li><li>While I see the appeal of …, I would still …</li></ul> |

## Degrees of certainty

| Confident | Careful |
|---|---|
| <ul><li>Clearly, … / Undoubtedly, …</li><li>There can be little doubt that …</li><li>It is evident that …</li><li>… is bound to …</li></ul> | <ul><li>It is likely / unlikely that …</li><li>… may / might / could …</li><li>… appears to / seems to …</li><li>Arguably, …</li><li>To some extent, …</li><li>This would suggest that …</li></ul> |

Hedging is not weakness. Overstating a claim you cannot support costs more than stating it carefully.

## Agreeing and disagreeing with a position

| Agreeing | Disagreeing |
|---|---|
| <ul><li>I share the view that …</li><li>There is a strong case for …</li><li>This argument is convincing because …</li><li>The author is right to point out that …</li></ul> | <ul><li>I find this argument unconvincing because …</li><li>This overlooks the fact that …</li><li>The evidence points the other way: …</li><li>This holds only if …, which is not the case here.</li></ul> |

## Comparing and evaluating

| Comparing | Weighing up |
|---|---|
| <ul><li>Both texts deal with …</li><li>Similarly, … / Likewise, …</li><li>… , whereas the second text …</li><li>The two differ mainly in …</li><li>Compared with …, …</li></ul> | <ul><li>The main advantage of … is …</li><li>The obvious drawback is …</li><li>This outweighs …</li><li>The strongest argument in favour is …</li><li>Weighing the two, …</li></ul> |

## Referring back and moving on

| Referring back | Moving on |
|---|---|
| <ul><li>As mentioned above, …</li><li>As shown earlier, …</li><li>Returning to …, …</li><li>This brings us back to …</li></ul> | <ul><li>Turning now to …</li><li>This raises the question of …</li><li>A second aspect worth considering is …</li><li>With this in mind, …</li></ul> |

## Closing a text

| Summing up | Leaving the reader with something |
|---|---|
| <ul><li>In conclusion, …</li><li>To conclude, …</li><li>To sum up, …</li><li>In summary, …</li><li>All things considered, …</li><li>On balance, …</li></ul> | <ul><li>Whether … remains to be seen.</li><li>The real question is not …, but …</li><li>If … is to change, … will have to …</li><li>What is needed above all is …</li></ul> |

!!! warning "A conclusion adds nothing new"

    New arguments in the final paragraph read as poor planning. Sum up, take your position, stop. One or two sentences of outlook are welcome, a fresh argument is not.

## Mediation: phrases that carry the source

| Naming the source | Explaining what a reader will not know |
|---|---|
| <ul><li>According to an article published in … on …, …</li><li>The article, which appeared in …, reports that …</li><li>As … explains in …, …</li></ul> | <ul><li>… , a German school-leaving qualification comparable to …</li><li>… , which in Germany means …</li><li>In Germany, … is organized by …</li><li>Unlike in the UK, …</li></ul> |

Cultural explanation is a marked category in mediation. A German institution dropped into an English text without a gloss loses content marks.
