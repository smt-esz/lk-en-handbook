# How to write a speech

!!! tip "At a glance"

    |   |   |
    |---|---|
    | **Purpose** | Move an audience towards **one message**: to think, feel or do something |
    | **Structure** | Attention-grabbing opening → body with about three points → memorable ending |
    | **Register & voice** | Neutral to formal, first person, address the audience directly with *we*, *us*, *you* |
    | **Length** | As set by the task. Spoken delivery: roughly 130 words per minute |
    | **Watch out** | Write for the ear, not the eye. Rhetorical devices must serve the message, not decorate it |

**Operators for this task type:** a speech is the single most requested text type on the whole exam, remember that.

| Operator | Example task |
|---|---|
| **write (a speech)** | *Write a speech for this conference reflecting on the relevance of historical events.* (Abitur 2010) |
| **write … comment (on)** | *Write the script of your speech in which you comment on this attitude towards literature.* (Abitur 2021) |

*write* sets the format, it is often combined with a second operator (usually *comment*) that sets what the speech has to argue. See [Operators](../language/operators.md#writing).

## Step by step

1. **Find your purpose.** What should the audience think, feel or do after your speech? Write that down in one sentence; it is your thesis.
2. **Know your audience.** Who are they, what do they care about, what do they already know? This decides your examples, your [register](../language/register-and-tone.md) and your opening.
3. **Grab attention at the start.** Options: a rhetorical question, a surprising fact, a short anecdote, a provocative statement. Then state your topic and why it matters to *this* audience.
4. **Build the body.** About three main points, each supported by a reason and a vivid example. Order them so the strongest comes last. Signal every transition ("This brings me to …").
5. **Make it persuasive.** Sound arguments first (logos), then add emotional appeal (pathos) and credibility (ethos). Devices that work in speeches: anaphora, rule of three, inclusive pronouns, contrast, imagery. See [stylistic devices](../language/stylistic-devices.md).
6. **End strongly.** Return to your opening, summarize your message in one sharp sentence, and close with an appeal or a thought the audience takes home.

## Useful phrases

| Purpose | Phrases |
|---|---|
| Opening | <ul><li>Have you ever wondered … ?</li><li>Imagine for a moment that …</li><li>Let me start with a simple fact: …</li><li>Two years ago, I …</li></ul> |
| Addressing the audience | <ul><li>Ladies and gentlemen, …</li><li>Fellow students, …</li><li>All of us know …</li><li>You and I have both …</li></ul> |
| Structuring | <ul><li>Let me make three points.</li><li>This brings me to my second point.</li><li>And that is precisely why …</li><li>So where does that leave us?</li></ul> |
| Involving the audience | <ul><li>We all know the feeling.</li><li>Ask yourselves: …</li><li>Together, we can …</li><li>Put yourself in their position.</li></ul> |
| Closing | <ul><li>So let us …</li><li>The choice is ours.</li><li>If you remember one thing from today, let it be this: …</li><li>Thank you.</li></ul> |

## Checklist for a speech

- [ ] One clear message, stated and repeated?
- [ ] Opening that actually grabs attention?
- [ ] About three supported points with signalled transitions?
- [ ] Audience addressed and included throughout?
- [ ] At least three rhetorical devices used deliberately?
- [ ] Strong closing line?

## Common mistakes

- Writing an essay and reading it out: no address, no "we", long written-style sentences
- Cramming in too many points instead of developing three
- Devices used at random rather than where they support the message
- A closing that trails off ("…and that's why I think it's important.")
