# How to write a mediation

!!! tip "At a glance"

    |   |   |
    |---|---|
    | **Purpose** | Transfer the content a specific **addressee** needs from a German text into English. Not a translation |
    | **Structure** | The structure of the **required text type**: email, article, blog post, letter, report |
    | **Register & voice** | Set by addressee and text type. Name your source. No opinion, no additions |
    | **Length** | As set by the task. Class rule of thumb: about **one quarter** of the source. In the final examination the source is max. 650 words |
    | **Watch out** | The **task decides** what is relevant, not the text. Never translate word for word, and never let German sentence patterns through |

**Operators for this task type:** mediation itself is not set by one operator, the addressee and text type do that. The instruction for *what* to transfer rotates between these:

| Operator | Example task |
|---|---|
| **summarize / sum up** — most common, remember this one | *Write a summary in English of what the following text says about children's values.* (Abitur 2011) |
| **outline** | *Outline what CityTrees are and why they might be interesting for big cities.* (Abitur 2019) |
| **present** | *Write an article for this website in which you present "Lieferrad DA".* (Abitur 2023) |

All of these ask for a concise account of the main points as the task specifies them. Where your addressee does not know a German institution or custom, you are expected to explain it.

See [Operators](../language/operators.md#mediation).

## Step by step

1. **Read the task first, then the text.** The task tells you the purpose, the addressee and the text type. Read the German text with the task in mind: what does *this* addressee need?
2. **Get an overview** (first reading). What is the topic, what are the core statements?
3. **Read closely** (second reading). Work through the text section by section. Mark the statements that answer the task; cross out examples and details that do not.
4. **Order the information.** Group related points and put them in a logical order. You do not have to follow the structure of the German text.
5. **Write the mediation.**

    - *Opening:* name the source (title, author, publication, date) and the topic, adapted to your text type. An email starts with a greeting and a reason for writing.
    - *Body:* neutral [register](../language/register-and-tone.md), no colloquial language, no personal opinion. Do not translate German idioms word by word; find an English way to say the same thing.
    - *Ending:* round the text off according to its type. An email needs a closing formula; a summary-style mediation simply ends with the last point.

6. **Revise.** Compare against the task, check the word count, correct the language.

## Useful phrases

| Purpose | Phrases |
|---|---|
| Referring to the source | <ul><li>The article "…" from … deals with …</li><li>According to an article published in … on …, …</li><li>As … explains in …, …</li></ul> |
| Reporting content | <ul><li>The author explains that …</li><li>As the text points out, …</li><li>It is reported that …</li><li>The article goes on to describe …</li></ul> |
| Explaining a German term | <ul><li>… , which is roughly the German equivalent of …</li><li>… , a German concept meaning …</li><li>In Germany, … is organized by …</li><li>Unlike in the UK, …</li></ul> |
| Email or letter frame | <ul><li>I am writing to you because …</li><li>I came across an article that might interest you.</li><li>I hope this helps. Let me know if you have any questions.</li></ul> |
| Article or website frame | <ul><li>If you are looking for an example from Germany, look at …</li><li>This is how it works in practice: …</li><li>What makes the idea work is …</li></ul> |

## Checklist for a mediation

- [ ] All aspects of the task covered, and only those?
- [ ] Text type conventions met (greeting, closing, headline …)?
- [ ] Word limit kept?
- [ ] Source named in the opening?
- [ ] No literal translations of German idioms or sentence patterns?
- [ ] No own opinion added?

## Common mistakes

- Translating sentence by sentence; the result sounds German and is far too long
- Including every detail instead of selecting for the task
- Forgetting the addressee: an email to a friend does not sound like an essay
- False friends (e.g. *aktuell* ≠ actual, *Chef* ≠ chef, *eventuell* ≠ eventually)
