# How to write a comment

!!! tip "At a glance"

    |   |   |
    |---|---|
    | **Purpose** | State **your opinion** on an issue and make a reader accept it |
    | **Structure** | Statement of opinion → arguments with evidence → conclusion |
    | **Register & voice** | Neutral to formal, first person, simple present |
    | **Length** | As set by the task, typically 200 to 300 words |
    | **Watch out** | A comment is **not a discussion**. You argue your side. Sitting on the fence fails the task |

**Operators for this task type:**

| Operator | Example task |
|---|---|
| **comment (on)** (AFB III) — the single most common operator on the whole exam, remember this one | *Comment on this proverb referring to different views presented in the text.* (Abitur 2008) |
| **evaluate** (AFB III) | *Evaluate Garth Brooks' vision of an ideal society and compare it to your personal opinion on the topic.* (Abitur 2008) |
| **justify** (AFB III) | On the official operator list, worth knowing even though it rarely turns up as the literal task word |

See [Operators](../language/operators.md#afb-iii-evaluating-and-creating).

## Step by step

1. **Collect ideas.** A quick mind map of pro and contra points helps you see which side you can argue more convincingly, even though you will mainly present one side.
2. **Take a position.** Decide before you write. A comment that sits on the fence fails its purpose.
3. **Write your introductory statement.** State the issue and your opinion on it, with a clear link to the text or question you were given.
4. **Build the argumentation.** Two to four arguments, each in its own paragraph: claim → reason → example or evidence. Order them from weakest to strongest, so your best point comes last.
5. **Deal with one counterargument.** Concede it briefly, then refute it ("Admittedly, … However, …"). This makes your position stronger, not weaker.
6. **Conclude.** Return to your opinion, sharpened by the arguments. Do not introduce new points here.

## Useful phrases

| Purpose | Phrases |
|---|---|
| Stating your opinion | <ul><li>In my opinion, …</li><li>I am convinced that …</li><li>It seems clear to me that …</li><li>I would argue that …</li></ul> |
| Giving reasons | <ul><li>The main reason is …</li><li>This is because …</li><li>… , which shows that …</li><li>The evidence for this is …</li></ul> |
| Giving examples | <ul><li>For instance, …</li><li>A case in point is …</li><li>This can be seen in …</li><li>Take … as an example.</li></ul> |
| Conceding and refuting | <ul><li>Admittedly, … However, …</li><li>It is true that …, but …</li><li>Critics may argue that …, yet …</li><li>There is something in this view, and still …</li></ul> |
| Concluding | <ul><li>To conclude, …</li><li>All things considered, …</li><li>For these reasons, I believe that …</li></ul> |

## Checklist for a comment

- [ ] Clear position stated in the first paragraph?
- [ ] Each argument in its own paragraph with reason and example?
- [ ] Arguments ordered weakest to strongest?
- [ ] One counterargument addressed and refuted?
- [ ] Conclusion matches the opinion you started with?

## Common mistakes

- Retelling the text instead of arguing about the issue
- Listing opinions without reasons or examples
- Switching sides in the middle of the text
- A conclusion that suddenly introduces a brand-new argument
