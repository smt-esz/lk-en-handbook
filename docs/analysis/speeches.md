# How to analyse a speech

!!! tip "At a glance"

    |   |   |
    |---|---|
    | **Purpose** | Show how a speaker persuades a particular audience on a particular occasion |
    | **Structure** | Communication situation → structure and argumentation → rhetorical means → evaluation |
    | **Register & voice** | Formal, neutral perspective, **simple present** |
    | **Length** | As set by the task |
    | **Watch out** | Naming a feature is never enough. Explain **how it works in context** and **why the speaker uses it**. Backbone: logos, pathos, ethos |

**Operators for this task type:** a speech as the main analysis text is rare on the actual exam, this is more the pattern from class.

| Operator | Example task |
|---|---|
| **analyse / assess**  | *Analyse Obama's speech and assess to what extent the skills described by Philip Collins in "Sounds good" apply.* (Abitur 2010) |
| **outline**, **examine**, **evaluate** | Standard operators for any rhetorical analysis, see [non-fictional texts](non-fictional-texts.md) |

See [Operators](../language/operators.md).

## Step by step

1. **Establish the communication situation.**

    - *Speaker:* Who is speaking? What do we know about them, and what is their position?
    - *Audience:* Who is listening? What do they expect, fear, hope for?
    - *Occasion:* What is the (political, historical, social) context of the speech?
    - *Purpose:* What does the speaker want to achieve?

2. **Outline content and structure.** What is the speech about, in brief? How is it organized: introduction, body, conclusion? Is there one governing idea that runs through the whole speech?
3. **Analyse the argumentation.** What are the central claims? How are they supported: facts, examples, authorities, shared values? Does the speaker address counterarguments?
4. **Analyse the modes of persuasion.**

    - *Logos:* logical arguments, evidence, facts and figures
    - *Pathos:* emotionally charged language, imagery, appeals to hopes and fears
    - *Ethos:* how the speaker builds credibility and connects to the audience's values

5. **Analyse the language.** Word choice (high or low style, inclusive pronouns, loaded terms), syntax (short or long sentences, parallelism, questions), and [stylistic devices](../language/stylistic-devices.md) typical of speeches: anaphora, rule of three, contrast, rhetorical questions, imagery. For each: quote, line, effect.
6. **Delivery, if you can see or hear the speech.** Voice, pauses, gestures, facial expressions; what is circumstantial and what is strategy?
7. **Evaluate.** Is the speech convincing? Does it fit occasion and audience? Is the balance of logos, pathos and ethos effective for its purpose?

## Useful phrases

| Purpose | Phrases |
|---|---|
| Situation | <ul><li>The speech was delivered by … on the occasion of …</li><li>… addresses an audience of …</li><li>The occasion itself matters, because …</li></ul> |
| Argumentation | <ul><li>The speaker's central claim is that …</li><li>To support this, he cites …</li><li>The argument builds from … towards …</li></ul> |
| Devices and effect | <ul><li>The anaphora "…" (ll. 4–6) hammers home …</li><li>By addressing the audience directly, the speaker …</li><li>The rule of three lends the passage …</li><li>The contrast between … and … sharpens …</li></ul> |
| Modes of persuasion | <ul><li>The speaker appeals to the audience's emotions by …</li><li>… establishes credibility by …</li><li>The logical backbone of the speech is …</li></ul> |
| Evaluation | <ul><li>The speech is effective because …</li><li>Whether this convinces the audience depends on …</li><li>The weakness of the speech lies in …</li></ul> |

## Checklist for analysing a speech

- [ ] Communication situation covered (speaker, audience, occasion, purpose)?
- [ ] Central claims and their support identified?
- [ ] Logos, pathos and ethos addressed?
- [ ] Every device quoted with line reference and effect?
- [ ] Final evaluation with reasons?

## Common mistakes

- Analysing the speech like an essay and ignoring audience and occasion
- Listing devices without connecting them to the speaker's purpose
- Summarizing the content at length instead of analysing the rhetoric
- An "evaluation" that only says "the speech is very good"
