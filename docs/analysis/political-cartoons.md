# How to analyse a political cartoon

!!! tip "At a glance"

    |   |   |
    |---|---|
    | **Purpose** | Decode the message a cartoonist makes with images rather than words, and judge how well it works |
    | **Structure** | Three strict steps in this order: **describe → analyse → evaluate** |
    | **Register & voice** | Formal, neutral perspective, **simple present** |
    | **Length** | As set by the task |
    | **Watch out** | No interpretation in the description. The message lives in the **gap** between what is shown and what it stands for. Always read captions, labels and speech bubbles |

**Operators for this task type:** a cartoon always appears as a short Part A2 task, the literal instruction is usually one line. describe → analyse → evaluate is the internal structure you supply yourself, not what the task spells out, keep that in mind.

| Operator | Example task |
|---|---|
| **discuss** — most common, remember this one | *Discuss the message of the cartoon.* (Abitur 2010) |
| **comment (on)** | *Based on a short analysis of the cartoon, comment on its message.* (Abitur 2019) |
| **assess** | *Using the message of the cartoon as a starting point, assess to what extent people's private lives will be affected by technological developments in the future.* (Abitur 2022) |

See [Operators](../language/operators.md).

## Step by step

1. **Description.** State the basics first: cartoonist, source and date if given, and the topic at first sight. Then describe systematically: foreground before background, left to right. Mention people (appearance, posture, facial expressions), objects, setting, and all text elements (caption, labels, speech bubbles). Simple present.
2. **Analysis.** Now decode what you described:

    - *Symbols:* What do the elements stand for? (e.g. figures standing for countries, a dove for peace)
    - *Caricature techniques:* What is exaggerated or distorted, and why? Are stereotypes used?
    - *Allusions:* What real events, people or debates does the cartoon refer to? Provide the background knowledge.
    - *Contrast and irony:* What clashes: words vs. image, size of figures, expectations vs. reality?

3. **The message.** Condense the analysis into one or two sentences: what is the cartoonist's opinion on the issue? A cartoon always takes a position; find it.
4. **Evaluation.** Does the cartoon make its point effectively? Is it fair or one-sided? Do you share its view? Support your judgement with reasons.

## Useful phrases

| Purpose | Phrases |
|---|---|
| Description | <ul><li>The cartoon by … deals with …</li><li>In the foreground, we can see …</li><li>In the background, …</li><li>The caption reads "…"</li></ul> |
| Analysis | <ul><li>The man on the left symbolizes …</li><li>… stands for …</li><li>The cartoonist alludes to …</li><li>By exaggerating …, the cartoonist …</li></ul> |
| Message | <ul><li>The cartoonist criticizes …</li><li>… mocks …</li><li>… draws attention to …</li><li>The message is that …</li></ul> |
| Evaluation | <ul><li>The cartoon makes its point effectively because …</li><li>In my view, the cartoon oversimplifies …</li><li>The image works because it needs no words to …</li></ul> |

## Checklist for analysing a political cartoon

- [ ] Description strictly separate from interpretation?
- [ ] All text elements (caption, labels, bubbles) included?
- [ ] Symbols and allusions explained with background knowledge?
- [ ] Message stated in one or two clear sentences?
- [ ] Evaluation with reasons, not just "I like it"?

## Common mistakes

- Interpreting in the first sentence ("The cartoon shows that politicians are liars")
- Overlooking the caption or the labels, and with them the point
- Describing every pencil stroke but never reaching the message
- Missing the background knowledge the cartoon alludes to; if you do not know the context, say what the elements suggest
