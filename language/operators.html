    Operators: what each task word means

    The operator tells you exactly what kind of answer is expected. Doing the wrong operation is one of the most expensive mistakes in a class exam: an analysis that only describes loses points even if everything in it is correct.

    The exam works with an official list of core operators. The tables below follow that list, in this order: writing, mediation, speaking. Operators outside the list can still appear when their everyday meaning is clear, so a short section at the end covers those too.

    At a glance
    Purpose Work out what operation a task word actually demands before you start writing or speaking
    Structure Core operators for writing, then mediation, then speaking, then a few others you may still meet
    Register & voice Not set by the operator. The text type decides that
    Length Not set by the operator either. The task and the printed weighting tell you how much to give each part
    Watch out Doing the wrong operation is the most expensive mistake in a class exam. An analysis that only describes loses marks even when everything in it is true

    Writing

    Operator What you have to do
    analyse, examine Say in detail how the text works and explain it, with evidence from the text. Connect what the text says to its language and form. The task usually names the aspects: Focus on structure and use of language
    assess, evaluate Reach a well-founded judgement on how valuable, effective or true something is, and back it up with evidence and examples
    comment (on) Give your own reasoned opinion on something, supported by evidence and examples. You may bring in counterarguments
    compare Explain what is similar and what differs, with evidence from the text, and tie it to language and form
    describe Give a detailed account of what a person or thing is like
    discuss Weigh the arguments for both sides of an issue and reach a reasoned opinion, backed by evidence and examples
    explain Make clear how or why something is as it is, by drawing on the text and its context
    outline, summarize, sum up Give a concise account of the main points or ideas, as the task specifies them
    point out Present the main aspects of something, briefly
    write (+ text type) Produce a text with the typical features of that type. A second operator in the task says what the text has to do

    Two things in this table deserve a second look.

    Some operators are one operator with two names. Analyse and examine ask for the same thing, and so do assess and evaluate. The same goes for outline, summarize and sum up. Do not look for a difference in depth between them, there is none.

    "Write" almost never comes alone. The task names the text type and then adds a second operator: Write a blog entry in which you assess to what extent …. The first operator sets the format, the second sets the thinking. Both are marked.

    Mediation

    Operator What you have to do
    outline, present, summarize, sum up Give a concise account of the main points as the task specifies them, and explain culture-related aspects where your addressee needs them
    write (+ text type) Produce the text type the task names, again explaining culture-related aspects where necessary

    The task decides what counts as a main point, not the German text. See How to write a mediation.

    Speaking

    Operator What you have to do
    agree on, come to an agreement Arrive at one shared opinion or decision, based on arguments
    analyse, examine Describe the material, explain it and support what you say from it
    argue Make a case for or against a given view, using suitable evidence
    assess, evaluate Give a well-founded judgement on the value, quality or significance of something, with arguments
    comment (on) Give a reasoned opinion, supported by arguments, and consider counterarguments where they help
    compare Explain what is similar and what differs
    describe Give a detailed account of what someone or something is like
    discuss Together with your partner: trade arguments for and against a view and draw a conclusion you can justify
    explain Make something clear with examples and, where needed, its wider context
    make suggestions Propose measures or initiatives and say why they should be taken
    relate (to) Show how two or more texts or ideas oppose, complement or contradict each other

    In the speaking exam discuss is a joint task. A good one-sided argument still loses marks if you never let your partner's points change anything. See The speaking exam.

    Other operators you may meet

    These are not on the official core list. A task may still use them when the everyday meaning is obvious, and class tasks use them often.

    Operator What you have to do
    characterize Describe a person's traits and show how the text makes them visible
    contrast Concentrate on the differences between two things
    illustrate Make a point clear with examples
    interpret Explain the deeper meaning of a text or one of its elements in a wider context
    justify Give solid reasons for a position or decision
    present Lay out information clearly and in order
    state Say something clearly and briefly, without discussion

    Read the whole task, not just the operator

    "Analyse how the author creates suspense" limits the analysis to suspense. The operator sets the operation, the rest of the task sets the focus. Answer exactly that, nothing else.

    Operators that look alike

    These pairs are easy to mix up. Mixing them up is the exact mistake this page exists to prevent.

    • describe vs. analyse: describe stays with what is there, analyse asks how it works and with what effect
    • discuss vs. comment: discuss weighs both sides before it reaches a verdict, comment argues your own opinion and uses counterarguments only to support it
    • outline vs. retell: outline gives the points the task asks about, in your own order and at a higher level than the text. It does not follow the text paragraph by paragraph
    • assess vs. comment: assess judges how far something works or holds true, comment gives your view on an issue
    • interpret vs. analyse: interpret reaches for the deeper meaning in a wider context, analyse stays with evidence and effect in the text itself
