    The written examination

    What the written English exam looks like, how long you have, and how the three parts are weighted against each other.

    At a glance
    Format Three parts: speaking 20 %, writing 55 %, mediation 25 %. Part A offers a choice of fictional or non-fictional source, Part B does not
    Timing Written parts together 285 minutes, choice and reading time included
    What counts Within each written part: language 60 %, content 40 %. Sub-task weightings are printed on your paper
    Watch out Two of the four topic fields change from year to year. Part B is a quarter of the grade for a fraction of the time

    Check the topic fields for your year

    The weightings and timings below are the current ones. The topic fields are the part that has changed before, so your teacher will confirm the list for your year in year 12.

    The three parts

    Part Competence What you do Weight
    Practical part Speaking Paired exam, focus on argumentation and interaction 20 %
    Part A Writing Text-based writing task. You choose between a fictional and a non-fictional source. Source material max. 1000 words 55 %
    Part B Mediation One task, no choice. German source into English, adapted to addressee, situation and text type. Source material max. 650 words 25 %

    The speaking exam is written up on its own page: The speaking exam.

    Timing

    Time
    Written parts A and B together 285 minutes, including choice time
    Speaking exam, two candidates about 20 minutes
    Speaking exam, three candidates about 25 minutes

    The 285 minutes include 15 minutes of extra choice time, granted because Part A gives you the fictional or non-fictional option. Reading and choosing is part of your working time, not a bonus before it.

    A workable split

    285 minutes is a lot of time and it disappears faster than you think. A rough plan, to adapt:

    Phase Minutes
    Read both Part A options, decide 20
    Part A: plan 25
    Part A: write 110
    Part B: read, plan, write 90
    Revise both, language pass 40

    Leave the language pass in. Language is 60 % of both written parts, and a focused final read is the highest-value forty minutes of the day.

    The sub-tasks in Part A

    Part A comes in three numbered sub-tasks. Sub-tasks 1 and 2 are the work with the text, called A1 in this handbook: first an outline, then an analysis. Sub-task 3 is the composition beyond the text, called A2. The percentage weighting of each sub-task is printed on your exam paper. Read it before you plan: it tells you where the content marks actually sit, and it is not always distributed the way you would guess.

    Sub-task 3 comes in two forms, both connected back to the text: a personal response or a piece of creative writing. Both are marked with the same rigour. Practise both, and where the paper lets you choose, take the one you have practised, not the one that looks shorter.

    Content: the four topic fields

    Part A assumes knowledge of four topic fields. You are not asked to reproduce them, but the texts and tasks are set inside them, and background knowledge shows in the quality of your comment.

    Two of the four fields change from year to year

    Fields 1 and 2 have been the same in every recent year. Fields 3 and 4 changed for the 2027 exam, and the fields for 2028 are not published yet. Which is why the course covers all the candidates rather than betting on two of them.

    2026 exam 2027 exam
    1 The individual and society The individual and society
    2 Politics, culture, society — USA Politics, culture, society — USA
    3 The media Politics, culture, society — UK
    4 Global chances and challenges Science and technology

    What each field covers

    Topic field Focus
    The individual and society Questions of identity: ambitions and obstacles, conformity vs. individualism. Chances and challenges for society: ethnic, cultural and social diversity, gender issues
    Politics, culture, society — USA From past to present: American ideals and realities, freedom, equality and the pursuit of happiness. Current issues: questions of identity, political, cultural and social developments
    Politics, culture, society — UK From past to present: the Elizabethan Age, the British Empire, postcolonial Britain. Current issues: questions of identity, political, cultural and social developments
    Science and technology Scientific and technological progress and its impact on society. Visions of the future: utopia vs. dystopia
    The media The changing media landscape: traditional and modern media. The impact of the media on the individual and society: information, entertainment, manipulation
    Global chances and challenges Working towards social, environmental and economic sustainability. International relations: conflict and cooperation, peacekeeping, migration

    The course covers more still, for instance the world of work and English as a world language. Those topics matter for mediation tasks, for your other assessment and for your general command of the language.

    Dictionaries and other aids

    Allowed Not allowed
    Monolingual English dictionary Anything with internet access
    Bilingual English–German and German–English dictionary Electronic devices whose memory can be extended
    German spelling dictionary Any dictionary in the speaking exam

    Electronic dictionaries are only allowed as closed systems. Any memory they have must be cleared or locked. Checking that your aids comply is your responsibility, and a breach can be treated as an attempted deception.

    Dictionaries are provided by the school. Bring your own only if you have cleared it beforehand.

    How the parts are combined

    Each of the three parts gets its own grade points. Within Part A and Part B, language counts 60 % and content 40 %. The three results are then weighted 55, 25 and 20 per cent and added up.

    Part A   (language 0.6 + content 0.4)   ×  0.55
         content = sub-tasks 1, 2 and 3, each weighted as printed on your paper
Part B   (language 0.6 + content 0.4)   ×  0.25
Speaking                                ×  0.20
──────────────────────────────────────────────
=  final grade points

    Read that once more with the mediation task in mind. Part B is a quarter of your exam grade and takes far less time than Part A. Per minute invested, it is the best-paid task in the paper, and it is the one candidates most often under-practise.

    The written exam is marked twice

    Your teacher marks first. A second teacher from another school marks independently and does not know whose paper it is. That is why no sheet may carry your name, only your school code and candidate number. See Exam day.
