    The speaking exam

    The practical part of the final English examination. It is a paired exam, it lasts about twenty minutes, and it is worth 20 % of your final English grade.

    At a glance
    Format Paired exam, partners drawn by lot, task set centrally. Focus on argumentation and interaction
    Timing About 20 minutes for two candidates, 25 for three. You learn your slot one school day in advance
    What counts Three equal criteria: content, interaction, language. No dictionaries, notes allowed
    Watch out Interaction is a third of the marks. Two parallel monologues lose in the same category as talking over your partner

    Format
    Setting Conversation between candidates, examiners listen and lead in
    Length about 20 minutes for two candidates, about 25 for three
    Task Set centrally, the same for every school in the state
    Focus Argumentation and interaction
    Aids None. No dictionaries
    Notes Brief notes during the conversation are allowed

    Pairs, and the order in which pairs are examined, are drawn by lot by the examination board. Pairings may cross courses. You are told when your slot is one school day beforehand. If your partner does not turn up, you are assigned to another group.

    The chair of the board introduces the task. You may ask what an individual word in the task means. Asking does not cost you marks. Use that, it is free.

    What is actually marked

    Three categories, weighted equally:

    Category The question behind it
    Handling the content Do you meet the task, cover the aspects of the topic with substance, and build a line of thought that convinces
    Interaction Do you react to your partner, take an active part and help steer the conversation, and use communication strategies such as paraphrasing, asking back, correcting yourself and body language
    Language Pronunciation, intonation and pace, plus range and accuracy of vocabulary and structures

    Interaction is a third of the marks, which is the part candidates most often forget. Two people delivering parallel monologues both lose in the same category. Talking over your partner loses you the same category from the other direction.

    Spoken language is marked as spoken language. Short or unfinished sentences, filler words, repetition and pauses are fine as long as they serve the conversation. Strategies for when words fail count in your favour: paraphrase, ask back, correct yourself.

    What good interaction sounds like

    Move Phrases
    Picking up a point That's a good point, and it makes me think of … / You mentioned … , which is exactly where I'd disagree
    Disagreeing constructively I see it differently, because … / I take your point, but consider …
    Inviting your partner in What's your take on …? / Would you go that far?
    Asking for clarification Sorry, do you mean … ? / Could you say more about … ?
    Building a joint conclusion So we both seem to think that … / Where we differ is …
    Buying yourself a second That's a difficult one. Let me think about it for a moment …
    Rescuing a sentence What I mean is … / Let me put it differently / Sorry, I meant …

    Learn six of these properly rather than twenty badly. They are what turns two monologues into a conversation.

    How to prepare

    1. Practise in pairs, not alone. The skill being tested only exists between two people.
    2. Rehearse disagreeing. Most candidates default to agreeing with everything their partner says, which produces a short, flat conversation and costs marks in both content and interaction.
    3. Build a stock of examples from the four topic fields. You will not be asked to reproduce facts, but a concrete example lifts an argument out of the general.
    4. Time yourself. Twenty minutes of continuous English feels long the first time and short by the fifth.
    5. Record one practice run and listen back. Uncomfortable, and the fastest way to notice your own filler habits.

    Checklist before the exam

    • I have practised in pairs, not alone
    • I can disagree with a partner in English without sounding rude
    • I know six interaction phrases by heart
    • I have two concrete examples ready for each topic field
    • I have spoken English for twenty minutes without stopping at least once
    • I have recorded myself once and listened back
    • I know that dictionaries are not allowed and have stopped relying on one

    On the day

    • Silence is more expensive than an imperfect sentence. If you are stuck, say so in English and hand over
    • If your partner dries up, ask them a question. That is not charity, it is marks in your interaction category
    • Notes are allowed, reading aloud from them is not the point. Keywords, not sentences
    • Everything is minuted throughout. The minutes may be kept in English
    • Your score is announced right after the exam and cannot be changed later by the people marking your written paper
